This open educational resource bridges rigorous theory and hands-on practice across cybersecurity,
cryptography, networking, offensive and defensive security, digital forensics, incident response,
governance, privacy and law, and emerging topics. It is layered to serve readers from high school through
postdoctoral level and is mapped to the CISSP, Security+, CEH, CISA, and CGRC certifications.

## How the Field Is Taught

Cybersecurity education joins foundational computer science to hands-on work on systems that are deliberately
broken, and the chapters that follow are ordered to match that progression.

**Core curriculum.** Three strands recur across programs. *Foundations* supply the prerequisites everything
else rests on: discrete mathematics, computer networks, operating systems, and programming in languages close
enough to the machine that memory layout and protocol behavior stay visible, usually Python, C, and assembly.
*Defensive and offensive skills* are taught together, because each explains the other: cryptography
(Chapter 2), secure software development and web security (Chapter 10), penetration testing (Chapters 6
through 9), network monitoring and defense (Chapters 11 and 12), and digital forensics (Chapter 13).
*Policy and ethics* frame both: data protection law such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and
the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in Chapter 18, governance frameworks from the National Institute of
Standards and Technology (NIST) and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 in Chapters 5 and 19, and the authorization boundary
that separates lawful security testing from unlawful intrusion, in Chapter 6.

**Teaching methods.** Practical work belongs inside an isolated environment, whether a local virtual machine, a
container, or an instructor-provisioned cloud account, so that a student can run malware or misconfigure a
firewall without touching a production network. Capture the flag competitions turn exercises into timed puzzles
in which a hidden token, the flag, proves that a vulnerability was found and exploited (Chapter 16). Cyber
range exercises scale the same idea to a whole class: a red team of attackers works against a blue team of
defenders in a simulated security operations center, which lets incident response (Chapter 14) be rehearsed
under time pressure rather than only read about. Appendix H collects capstone and group project ideas, and
Appendix K collects sample assignments and course materials.
